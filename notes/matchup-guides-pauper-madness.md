## Matchup 1: Mono Red Madness (Mirror)

**Record: 16-8 (67%)**

### Their Gameplan
The same as yours: flood the board with cheap damage triggers, discard to madness/draw, and close with Fireblast. The opponent also runs Kessig Flamebreather, Guttersnipe, Voldaren Epicure, Faithless Looting, Fiery Temper, and Lava Dart. The differences between lists determine who has the edge.

### Your Gameplan
Race, but be surgical about it. The mirror is decided by who lands Guttersnipe first and protects it, who uses Fireblast as a finisher rather than a panic button, and who keeps better hands. Kessig Flamebreather and Guttersnipe are the kings here — they convert your spell-heavy strategy into a decisive advantage if your opponent can't answer them.

### Role Assignment
Both players are trying to be the beatdown. The player who resolves Guttersnipe first and untaps with it usually wins. If you're behind on board, do NOT race — reassign to blocker control. Lava Dart (and its flashback) is the premier mirror tool because it's free recursion that kills Voldaren Epicure and trades with opposing Lava Darts without costing you spell slots.

**Postboard:** Role doesn't flip, but you're both adding hate. Relic of Progenitus disrupts Faithless Looting flashback and Lava Dart flashback — the two biggest sources of free value in this matchup.

### Key Cards

**Theirs that hurt you:**
- Guttersnipe — if they stick it before you do, you're racing 2 damage a spell and falling behind fast
- Fireblast — their finisher, same as yours. Never tap out in a way that lets Fireblast win on the spot
- Lava Dart (flashback) — free 1 damage to kill your 1/1s (Epicure, Sneaky Snacker if it somehow matters)

**Yours that matter most:**
- Guttersnipe — best card in the mirror. Protect it. It goes in before Kessig because at 3 toughness it survives Lava Dart
- Fireblast — the mirror-ending finisher. Hoard your Mountains. Don't sacrifice them carelessly
- Lava Dart (flashback) — kills Voldaren Epicure, trades with their Lava Darts, 0 mana to activate

### Key Interactions
- **Guttersnipe (3/2) survives Lava Dart (1 damage) and Voldaren Epicure ETB (1 damage).** Oracle: Guttersnipe has no additional toughness clause — it's just base 3 toughness, so a single Lava Dart won't kill it. Confirmed.
- **Kessig Flamebreather (2/2) dies to Lava Dart.** Oracle: Kessig has 2 toughness. Lava Dart deals 1 damage to "any target" — the creature takes 1, survives once, but dies if they flashback the same Dart from the graveyard (sacrifice a Mountain for flashback). Net cost: they sacrifice a Mountain for 2 damage to Kessig.
- **Grab the Prize triggers Kessig/Guttersnipe** — oracle: Grab the Prize is a noncreature spell, so Kessig triggers; it's a sorcery, so Guttersnipe triggers. Discard a card to cast it → if discarded card is Fiery Temper, Fiery Temper goes to madness exile and you cast it off madness = SECOND trigger on each. Two spells, two triggers. This is a real line.
- **Sneaky Snacker:** Oracle says "When you draw your third card in a turn." Faithless Looting draws 2, Highway Robbery draws 2, Grab the Prize draws 2 — all can enable Snacker in the graveyard. But Snacker returns tapped, so it doesn't block immediately. Mostly relevant as a free body that attacks next turn.
- **Relic of Progenitus:** The `{T}` mode exiles one card from ONE player's graveyard. The `{1}` + exile mode exiles ALL graveyards. In the mirror, use it to strip flashback on Faithless Looting and Lava Dart. However, it also nukes YOUR Lava Dart flashback. Time it to gain maximum asymmetry — activate after you've already used your Lava Dart flashbacks.

### Sideboard
**Bring in:** 3 Relic of Progenitus  
**Cut:** 1 Sneaky Snacker, 1 Highway Robbery, 1 Guttersnipe (keep 2 Guttersnipes)

**Why:** Relic cuts off Lava Dart flashback and Faithless Looting flashback — the mirror's two biggest free-value engines. Cut a Sneaky Snacker because it requires drawing 3 cards and returns tapped, which is too slow; the mirror is usually over before it matters. Cut a Highway Robbery — at 2 mana it's the slowest discard outlet and you want your early turns focused on threats. Keep all 3 Guttersnipes — they're your best threat.

**Do NOT bring in:** Smash to Smithereens (no relevant artifacts in the mirror), End the Festivities (your own creatures die to it too — you're not trying to sweep), Searing Blaze (they don't always have creatures to target and the landfall condition is hard to hit consistently).

### Mulligan Philosophy
Keep any hand with 2–3 lands and at least one 1-mana threat (Epicure) or Flamebreather + any spell. You need to start applying pressure by turn 1. Hands with 4+ spells and 1 land are too fragile — one mana stumble and you lose the race. The ideal opener has: Mountain, Voldaren Epicure, Faithless Looting (or Fiery Temper), and any burn spell. 

Mulligan hands that are all spells and 0 lands. Mulligan hands that are 4+ lands. Mulligan hands with 2 Guttersnipes but no early plays — Guttersnipe is a 3-drop and you die before it matters.

### Win Condition
Get to 7–10 damage from creatures and triggers by turn 3, then Fireblast (sacrificing two Mountains you're done using) for the last 4. Or: Guttersnipe + 3 spells = 6 damage, plus incidental creature damage puts them in lethal range.

### Rough Win %
**67%** (confirmed from 24 match data set). Favored. Your deck is well-tuned for the mirror.

## Matchup 2: Blue Terror (Mono-Blue / Dimir Terror)

**Record: 0-2 (0%)**

### Their Gameplan
Dump instants and sorceries in the graveyard via Mental Note, Preordain, Pieces of the Puzzle, and Lorien Revealed to make Tolarian Terror a cheap (often 1–2 mana) 5/5 with Ward {2}. Then counter your win attempts with Counterspell and Circular Logic. Dimir versions add Snuff Out (4 life, free), Ninja of the Deep Hours for extra card draw, and Gurmag Angler as a second threat.

**Key cards:** 
- Tolarian Terror (`{6}{U}`, costs `{1}` less per instant/sorcery in GY — can be 1–2 mana, Ward {2})
- Counterspell (`{U}{U}`)
- Circular Logic (`{2}{U}`, Madness `{U}` — counter unless controller pays {1} per card in their GY)
- Snuff Out (`{3}{B}`, pay 4 life if control a Swamp — destroy target nonblack creature)
- Ninja of the Deep Hours (`{3}{U}`, Ninjutsu `{1}{U}` — draw a card on combat damage)
- Gurmag Angler (`{6}{B}`, Delve — 5/5, often 1–2 mana)

### Your Gameplan
This is your worst matchup in terms of pure card advantage. They generate resources faster, they protect threats better, and their win conditions invalidate your burn. Tolarian Terror has Ward {2} — every removal spell costs them 0 but costs you an extra 2 mana tax if you somehow try to target it (you can't, it just hits players). The real problem: your damage spells all go face, but their permission stops your key threats (Kessig, Guttersnipe), and the Terrors end the game before you can race.

Your only path: go so fast that their permission arrives a turn late. T1 Epicure (1 damage ETB), T2 Kessig Flamebreather, T3 Faithless Looting + Fiery Temper via madness = massive damage before they can stabilize. You are not playing a long game here. Every top-deck past turn 5 is probably a loss.

### Role Assignment
Pure beatdown. They're the control deck. You have no path as a control player. Do not sandbag burn — point everything at them from turn 1. Do not wait to set up Guttersnipe for extra value. You are trying to kill them before they tap out for a 2-mana 5/5 with Ward.

**Postboard:** Still beatdown. You don't gain tools that fundamentally change this.

### Key Cards

**Theirs that hurt you:**
- Counterspell — stops your Guttersnipe and Kessig Flamebreather when you need them most
- Circular Logic — their in-hand counter is free or near-free when they have a full graveyard
- Tolarian Terror — a 5/5 for 1–2 mana blocks your entire team and attacks for lethal in 2 swings
- Snuff Out (Dimir only) — kills your Guttersnipe or Flamebreather at instant speed, free, going on their turn

**Yours that matter most:**
- Voldaren Epicure — uncounterable on T1 (they're not holding up Counterspell yet), 1 damage, creates a Blood token for another discard outlet
- Fireblast — uncounterable in a significant way. You sacrifice two Mountains as an alternative cost; this is a cast trigger with no targeting before resolution. They can counter it, but it's your best "through permission" option since it's an instant. If they're at 4 or less, don't run it into open mana unless you're sure
- Lava Dart (flashback) — cannot be countered once in graveyard by Circular Logic (Logic only counters spells being cast, not flashback activations)... wait, Lava Dart flashback IS casting the card. Circular Logic can counter it. However, Lava Dart off graveyard still only costs a Mountain sac, not mana, so it's free pressure. Confirmed: Lava Dart flashback is casting from graveyard and CAN be targeted by Circular Logic. But the {U} madness cost of Circular Logic means they need blue mana available.

### Key Interactions
- **Tolarian Terror Ward {2}:** Oracle says "Whenever this creature becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, counter it unless that player pays {2}." You are not targeting it with burn — you're burning the player. Ward is irrelevant to your game plan. But it means your creatures can't race through it — they need to go around or burn the player out before Terror beats them down.
- **Circular Logic Madness {U}:** Oracle confirms — it's a madness card. When they discard it (via Faithless Looting, Careful Study, Mental Note to graveyard then looted), it goes to madness exile and can be cast for `{U}`. With a graveyard full of instants/sorceries it counters at essentially `{U}` + pay 0 (if you can't afford X). This means your 1-mana spells get countered for a net cost advantage on their side. Very punishing.
- **Snuff Out targets nonblack creatures.** All your creatures (Kessig, Guttersnipe, Epicure, Sneaky Snacker) are nonblack. They can all be Snuffed Out for 4 life. Irrelevant to your game plan only insofar as their life total doesn't matter — you're burning them from 20 anyway.

### Sideboard
**Bring in:** 0 (nothing in your sideboard meaningfully helps here)  
**Cut:** Nothing (you're not boarding)

**Why:** Your 15-card sideboard has Smash to Smithereens (no artifacts to hit), Relic of Progenitus (disrupts their graveyard — actually DECENT here; Tolarian Terror gets bigger with more cards in GY, Relic slows that), End the Festivities (they have 0–1 creatures; useless), Searing Blaze (need them to control a creature; Terror is usually their only creature).

**Revised call:** Bring in 2 Relic of Progenitus if playing against Dimir Terror specifically. Relic's `{T}` mode forces them to exile one card from their graveyard each turn, slowing Tolarian Terror's cost reduction. Use the `{1}` pop strategically — don't pop it early, let it put pressure on their graveyard count over several turns before going wide.

**Cut:** 1 Sneaky Snacker, 1 Highway Robbery (slowest non-synergy pieces)

**Want but don't have:** Pyroblast / Red Elemental Blast would be an auto-4-of here. When you get them, bring all 4, cut 2 Guttersnipe and 2 Sneaky Snacker.

### Mulligan Philosophy
Extremely aggressive mulligan threshold. Keep any hand with 3+ damage sources that can apply pressure turns 1–3. A hand of Epicure + Bolt + Fiery Temper + Mountain + Faithless Looting + 2 more Mountains is a keep. A hand of Guttersnipe + lots of spells but no turn-1 or turn-2 plays is a mull — Guttersnipe on T3 into open Counterspell is a disaster.

If you're on the draw, an extra land and a madness outlet are worth more than usual since you need to resolve things before they're set up.

### Win Condition
Deal 20 before they assemble Tolarian Terror + counter backup. Realistically you need 8–12 from creatures and ETBs, then finish with Bolt + Fireblast (7 damage) in one turn through any counters. The "one big turn" is your only realistic path in a slow game.

### Rough Win %
**25–30%.** The 0-2 record matches theory. Your deck is structurally disadvantaged: their counters cost the same or less than your spells, they generate card advantage on par with yours, and their threats block efficiently. Without Pyroblast you're hoping they stumble. Play tight, go fast, accept the bad rate.

## Matchup 3: Elves

**Record: No data (first appearance at this meta share)**

### Their Gameplan
Flood the board with Elf bodies using Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, and Nettle Sentinel (untaps on green spells), generate enormous mana with Priest of Titania, and go wide with Lys Alana Huntmaster (creates a 1/1 token whenever you cast an Elf spell). Close the game with Distant Melody to refuel, or just attack with a board of 6+ creatures. They can kill on turn 3–4 with explosive draws.

**Key cards:**
- Llanowar Elves / Elvish Mystic (`{G}` — `{T}: Add {G}`)
- Nettle Sentinel (`{G}` — untaps on any green spell, creates mana loops with Priest)
- Priest of Titania (`{1}{G}` — `{T}: Add {G}` per Elf on battlefield)
- Lys Alana Huntmaster (`{2}{G}{G}` — each Elf spell creates a 1/1 token)
- Distant Melody (`{3}{U}` — draw a card per permanent of a chosen type)

### Your Gameplan
Kill their mana dorks before they get out of control, then close with face damage. Elves is a "critical mass" deck — if you let them untap with Priest of Titania and 4+ Elves, they refuel off Distant Melody for 5+ cards and you can never catch up. The early turns are everything. Bolt the Llanowar Elves. Bolt the Priest of Titania. Lava Dart the 1/1 tokens to reduce the board count.

End the Festivities (SB) is a one-card board reset that kills every 1/1 Elf token on the board simultaneously and hits the player. This is the best card in your sideboard for this matchup.

### Role Assignment
You're the aggressive player but you need to play reactive early. This is unusual for burn. Think of it as: spend mana 1–2 killing their acceleration, then play your threats turns 2–3, then burn face turns 4+. If you just jam face from turn 1 and ignore Priest of Titania, you die to Distant Melody for 8 cards.

**Postboard:** Same role. You get End the Festivities which speeds up the board-reset line.

### Key Cards

**Theirs that hurt you:**
- Priest of Titania — must die on sight. If she untaps with 4 Elves, they have 4 mana free to cast Distant Melody
- Distant Melody — the refuel. If it resolves with 4+ Elves in play, they draw 4+ cards and the game is over
- Nettle Sentinel — a 2/2 for `{G}` that untaps freely, blocks Epicure and Sneaky Snacker all day

**Yours that matter most:**
- Lightning Bolt — versatile kill on Priest, Huntmaster, Nettle Sentinel
- Lava Dart (and flashback) — kills every 1/1 Elf token. Two shots (regular + flashback) for the cost of one Mountain = removes two blockers
- End the Festivities (SB) — `{R}`, 1 damage to each opponent and each creature/planeswalker they control. Oracle confirmed: this hits the player AND all their creatures for 1. Kills every 1/1 token in play. Against a token-flood board this is a one-mana board wipe.
- Searing Blaze (SB) — 1+1 to player and creature, or 3+3 with landfall. The landfall condition is hard to hit (you're playing 18 Mountains, no fetch lands), but even 1+1 is a 2-for-1 against a token.

### Key Interactions
- **End the Festivities hits ALL their creatures for 1.** Oracle: "deals 1 damage to each opponent and each creature and planeswalker they control." Confirmed — every Elf on their board takes 1, including tokens. A Nettle Sentinel (2/2) survives. A Lys Alana Huntmaster (2/2) survives. But the army of 1/1 tokens dies entirely.
- **Kessig Flamebreather triggers on noncreature spells.** Elves is mostly creatures. Kessig Flamebreather still triggers off your spells, but their deck gives it few extra triggers from their side. This is fine — Kessig still pings from your own spells.
- **Priest of Titania adds `{G}` per Elf on the battlefield** (not just yours). If they have 5 Elves, Priest taps for 5. This is the mana engine. Kill her immediately.
- **Lys Alana Huntmaster:** "Whenever you cast an Elf spell, you may create a 1/1 green Elf Warrior creature token." Oracle confirmed. This creates tokens that also trigger Huntmaster on future casts (since tokens are Elves). Do not let Huntmaster sit. She snowballs.

### Sideboard
**Bring in:** 4 End the Festivities, 3 Searing Blaze  
**Cut:** 3 Guttersnipe, 2 Highway Robbery, 2 Sneaky Snacker

**Why:** End the Festivities is a mass -1 toughness effect for `{R}`. It's the best possible card against a 1/1 token army. Searing Blaze removes a creature AND pings the player — a 2-for-1 in a matchup where tempo is everything. Cut Guttersnipe because at 3 mana you're often dead or they've flooded the board before you can leverage it; plus it dies to Snuff Out (if Golgari) and the sorcery-speed spells you'd use to trigger it are slower than the instant-speed pressure you need. Cut Highway Robbery (slow, 2-mana discard outlet isn't needed when you want mana for kill spells). Cut Sneaky Snacker (needs 3 cards drawn to trigger, doesn't apply pressure early, 2 mana but off-color for a mono-red deck in game 1 viability).

### Mulligan Philosophy
You want Lightning Bolt, Lava Dart, or End the Festivities in your opening hand. A hand without any interaction against their mana dorks is too risky — they can goldfish you on turn 3. Keep hands with: 2 land, Bolt or Lava Dart, 2 burn spells. You can mulligan to 6 looking for an interactive hand; Elves can't race you if you strip their acceleration.

### Win Condition
Kill their mana acceleration, apply creature-based damage (Epicure, Kessig), clear their token army with End the Festivities, then Bolt + Fireblast for the close.

### Rough Win %
**55–60%.** No personal data, but the matchup calculus is favorable: Elves dies to mass 1-damage effects, you have 4 of those in your sideboard. Their creatures are all small enough for Lava Dart, Lightning Bolt, and Searing Blaze. This is a good matchup postboard. G1 is more even since they can go wide before you can interact.

## Matchup 4: Jund Wildfire

**Record: No data**

### Their Gameplan
Jund Wildfire is a recursive value deck built around sacrificing artifacts (primarily Ichor Wellspring) to draw cards via Fanatical Offering and Deadly Dispute, while recurring cards from the graveyard with Rotten Reunion and Golgari Brownscale (Dredge 2). The "Wildfire" angle comes from using powerful sorceries and creatures to stabilize and grind. Avenging Hunter (5/4 Trample, takes the initiative) is a top-end threat. Cast Down and Defile handle your creatures.

**Key cards:**
- Fanatical Offering (`{1}{B}` — sac artifact/creature: draw 2, make a Map token)
- Ichor Wellspring (`{2}` — draw on ETB and when put in GY from battlefield)
- Cast Down (`{1}{B}` — destroy target nonlegendary creature)
- Defile (`{B}` — target creature gets -1/-1 for each Swamp you control)
- Avenging Hunter (`{4}{G}` — 5/4 Trample, takes initiative on ETB)
- Golgari Brownscale (Dredge 2 — when returned to hand, gain 2 life)

### Your Gameplan
Race them before they set up their value engine. Jund Wildfire's draws slow down as they spend mana on draw spells; their fast turn is around turn 4–5 with Avenging Hunter. You should be threatening lethal by turn 4 at latest. Ignore their lifegain from Brownscale — Dredge is a value mechanic not a lifegain one; you race through 2-life per Brownscale return easily. Focus on dealing damage before Avenging Hunter blocks with its giant body.

Critically: their removal (Cast Down, Defile) kills your creatures. Don't over-invest in creatures. Spread damage across direct burn and creature attacks.

### Role Assignment
Beatdown from the jump. They're the engine deck. You're the clock. Don't slow down.

**Postboard:** Same, but you can disrupt their artifact sacrifice engine.

### Key Cards

**Theirs that hurt you:**
- Cast Down — kills Kessig and Guttersnipe, your most important creatures
- Defile — at `{B}` with multiple Swamps, can kill a Kessig Flamebreather or Voldaren Epicure (-2/-2 or more)
- Avenging Hunter — 5/4 Trample stops all your ground attacks and wins the air/trample race

**Yours that matter most:**
- Fireblast — close the game before Hunter comes down
- Guttersnipe — if it sticks, every spell is 2 damage
- Lava Dart (flashback) — kills Golgari Brownscale when it's a 1/1 on board, forces them to use a removal spell

### Key Interactions
- **Defile math:** Defile deals -1/-1 for each Swamp they control. A typical Jund build runs 5–7 Swamps. That's -5/-5 to -7/-7 at peak mana. All your creatures die. Don't let them hit your Guttersnipe. Play creatures into Defile-safe windows (when they're tapped or at 1–2 Swamps early).
- **Ichor Wellspring double draw:** Oracle says it draws on ETB AND when put into graveyard from battlefield. Fanatical Offering says "sacrifice an artifact or creature: draw 2, create Map token." If they sacrifice Wellspring to Fanatical Offering, they draw from the Offering's effect AND from Wellspring's "put in GY" trigger. That's 4 cards off one sacrifice. Their card advantage engine is real.
- **Smash to Smithereens on Ichor Wellspring:** Oracle — destroy target artifact, deal 3 to its controller. If you target Ichor Wellspring with Smash to Smithereens, it gets destroyed (goes to GY), which triggers Wellspring's draw — they STILL get their draw, but they take 3 damage. This is still a good trade: 2 mana from you, 3 damage to them, they draw 1 (Wellspring GY trigger). Acceptable.

### Sideboard
**Bring in:** 4 Smash to Smithereens  
**Cut:** 2 Sneaky Snacker, 1 Highway Robbery, 1 Guttersnipe

**Why:** Smash to Smithereens hits Ichor Wellspring, Map tokens (from Fanatical Offering), and any other artifacts for 3 damage. It disrupts their draw engine and deals direct damage simultaneously. Cut Sneaky Snacker (too slow, off-color, the 3-card-draw threshold is hard to hit). Cut a Highway Robbery (2 mana too slow when you're racing their 4-drop). Cut a Guttersnipe because at 3 mana you're often not untapping with it against their instant-speed removal.

### Mulligan Philosophy
Keep hands that go wide in the first 3 turns. Two lands, Epicure, Bolt, Fiery Temper is ideal. You need to be at 8–12 damage before they hit turn 4. Mulligan pure-spell hands without creatures — you need bodies in play because direct burn alone can't race the lifegain.

### Win Condition
Get to 12+ damage by turn 4 via Epicure ETB, Kessig triggers, and direct burn, then Fireblast or Bolt for the last push before Avenging Hunter comes down.

### Rough Win %
**50–55%.** No data, but structurally even. Their engine is slow to set up; you can race it. Their removal is annoying but not overwhelming. A coin flip that swings toward you with proper sequencing.

## Matchup 5: Grixis Affinity

**Record: 3-1 (75%)**

### Their Gameplan
Deploy artifact-lands and zero-cost or free artifacts (Frogmite, Myr Enforcer via Affinity for artifacts), generate card advantage with Thoughtcast, Deadly Dispute, and Ichor Wellspring, and win with Galvanic Blast (4 damage with Metalcraft) or Cranial Plating on an unblockable creature. They can goldfish turns 4–5 with consistent hands.

**Key cards:**
- Galvanic Blast (`{R}` — 2 damage, 4 with Metalcraft — 3+ artifacts)
- Thoughtcast (`{4}{U}` — Affinity: often `{U}` or free, draw 2)
- Frogmite (`{4}` — Affinity: often 0 mana)
- Myr Enforcer (`{7}` — Affinity: often 1–2 mana, 4/4)
- Springleaf Drum (`{1}` — tap creature to add one mana of any color)
- Cranial Plating (`{2}` — +1/+0 per artifact you control, equip `{B}{B}`)
- Makeshift Munitions (`{1}{R}` — `{1}`, sac artifact/creature: 1 damage to any target)
- Disciple of the Vault (if in list — pings for each artifact put in GY)
- All That Glitters (`{1}{W}` — +1/+1 per artifact and/or enchantment you control)

### Your Gameplan
Race their clock. Affinity is fast but glass-cannon on card count — if you pick off their key permanents, they brick. Without Galvanic Blast (you don't have artifact-lands), you can't one-shot their Myr Enforcer, but Smash to Smithereens hits any artifact for 3 damage to them AND removes the artifact. Critically: Smash targets artifact-lands too. Hitting a Seat of the Synod or Vault of Whispers removes their Metalcraft count AND deals 3 damage.

Your creatures don't race their Enforcer (4/4 blocks everything). Focus on reaching lethal via direct damage while using Smash to deny their Metalcraft count.

### Role Assignment
Beatdown. They're also beatdown but slower to reach full power. You're faster in the first 3 turns; they're faster by turn 4–5 with Metalcraft active. Race before Metalcraft comes online.

**Postboard:** Same, but more disruptive. Smash to Smithereens becomes a core spell.

### Key Cards

**Theirs that hurt you:**
- Myr Enforcer — 4/4 that costs nothing or near-nothing. Impossible for your creatures to trade with
- Galvanic Blast — kills your Guttersnipe and Kessig Flamebreather at Metalcraft for 4 damage
- Makeshift Munitions — turns their artifact-land/token army into a machine gun against your creatures

**Yours that matter most:**
- Smash to Smithereens — destroys artifact (including artifact-lands), deals 3 to controller. Best card in your sideboard for this matchup
- Lightning Bolt — use it on their Disciple of the Vault (if present) or straight to face
- Fireblast — game-ending finisher before Metalcraft stabilizes

### Key Interactions
- **Smash to Smithereens on artifact-lands:** Oracle — "Destroy target artifact. Smash to Smithereens deals 3 damage to that artifact's controller." Artifact-lands (Seat of the Synod, Vault of Whispers) are artifacts. Smash destroys them AND deals 3 damage. This reduces their Metalcraft count (often below the 3-artifact threshold) and deals 3 damage. A 2-for-1 in your favor. Confirmed.

## Matchup 10: Dimir Faeries

Your only path: flood them with multiple 1-mana spells in one turn to overwhelm their Sprite count, or resolve Guttersnipe and pray it lives for one turn of triggers.

### Role Assignment
Beatdown, but awkward. Their tempo plan means they're not purely control — Ninja of the Deep Hours attacks for card advantage, Faeries build a board. You need to go under their counter suite while not letting their flyers race you.

**Postboard:** Still beatdown. Nothing in your sideboard fundamentally changes this.

### Key Cards

**Theirs that hurt you:**

- Snuff Out — kills Guttersnipe and Kessig Flamebreather for free (4 life, not mana)

- **Circular Logic Madness:** They discard it (e.g., via Ninja returning to hand then being discarded?) — actually they use their own discard outlets. With a full graveyard, Circular Logic for `{U}` counters anything unless you pay (number of cards in their GY) × `{1}`. This means your Fireblast gets countered for `{U}` if they have 5 cards in GY. Very efficient.

**Cut:** 1 Sneaky Snacker, 1 Highway Robbery

**Why:** Relic slows down their graveyard count (reduces Circular Logic power, disrupts Ninja of the Deep Hours enablers). It's not transformative, but it's the best option you have. Everything else in your sideboard doesn't answer their game plan.

**Want but don't have:** 4 Pyroblast / Red Elemental Blast would be the correct answer here. They counter Counterspell, Spellstutter Sprite, Circular Logic, and their Faeries for `{R}`. Until you have them, this matchup is a known loss. Plan for it at deck construction, not at the table.

### Mulligan Philosophy
Take any 7 that applies pressure turns 1–2. Don't fish for silver bullets you don't have. Keep: Epicure + 2 burn spells + 2 Mountains. Play fast. Concede game 2 quickly if you're losing — don't burn mental energy on a structural loss. Preserve your time for matchups you can influence.

### Win Condition

Ideal line: T1 Epicure (1 damage, ETB uncounterable) → T2 Kessig + Lava Dart (1 trigger + 1 damage = 2 more) → T3 Faithless Looting (discard Fiery Temper) + Fiery Temper madness = Kessig fires twice + Temper deals 3 to face. That's potentially 10 damage by T3. Fireblast for 4 on T3 or T4 = 14 damage. One more spell closes it.

### Rough Win %
**20–25%.** The 0-2 record is small sample but directionally correct. This is a structural loss without Pyroblast. Play tight, go fast, accept the variance. Prioritize buying Pyroblast/Red Elemental Blast as your next purchase.
